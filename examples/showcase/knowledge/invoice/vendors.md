# Vendor onboarding and bank changes

## Onboarding a vendor

A vendor is paid only once it is in the vendor master as approved. To add one, purchasing sends:

- The legal name, address and tax form (W-9 for US vendors).
- The payment terms agreed in the contract (for example Net 30).
- The bank account for payments, confirmed by a call to a phone number we already hold, not one printed on the form.

The controller approves the vendor. Until then its invoices fail the "vendor approved" rule and go to the controller as exceptions.

## Bank account changes are the main fraud risk

Fraudsters pose as vendors and send a "new bank details" letter or email, so that the next payment goes to them. Treat every bank change as suspect.

- Never change a bank account because an invoice, email or letter says so.
- Confirm the change by calling the vendor on the phone number in the vendor master. Do not use contact details from the request itself.
- A second person approves the change. The vendor's bank account on file is shown with its last four digits only.
- After a change, the first payment waits for the controller's approval, even for a matched invoice.

If an invoice shows bank details that differ from the vendor master, put it on hold and tell the controller. Do not pay it.

## Other signs of a false invoice

- A vendor we have never ordered from, or a PO number that does not exist.
- Pressure to pay urgently or to skip the usual approval.
- Round totals, or lines that do not match what the warehouse received.
- An invoice number that repeats an earlier one with a different amount.
