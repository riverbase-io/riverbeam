# How a claim works

## 1. First notice of loss

You tell us what happened: the date, where it happened, what happened, who else was involved, the police report number if one was made, and whether the car can be driven. You can do this in the claims chat. We save your answers on a draft claim and file it when the facts are complete.

## 2. The damage estimate

Send a photo of the damage or the repair estimate from a body shop. The estimate is read for you: the damaged parts, whether each is repaired or replaced, labor hours and the total. You can correct anything that was read wrongly before the claim is filed.

## 3. Review

An adjuster reviews every filed claim, usually within **two business days**. The review checks that:

- the policy was active on the day of the loss;
- the cause of the loss is not excluded;
- the repair estimate minus the deductible is within the policy limit;
- a repair estimate is on file.

The adjuster also looks at signals that call for a closer look, for example a loss within 30 days of the policy start, a collision with another driver and no police report, or an estimate far above the usual cost for that level of damage. A signal is not an accusation. It means the adjuster checks before paying.

## 4. The decision

The adjuster approves the claim, approves part of it, denies it with a written reason, or sends it to investigation when more facts are needed. You are told the decision and the reason.

## 5. Payout

An approved claim is paid as **the repair estimate minus your deductible**. A second step requires the adjuster to approve the payment itself. Payment goes to you or to the repair shop, and normally arrives within **3 to 5 business days** after approval. A partially approved claim is paid the approved amount.

## If you disagree

Ask for a review with the adjuster's name on the claim. Send any new estimate or documents, and the adjuster looks at the claim again.
